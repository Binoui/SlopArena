# Industrial Rooftop Design Brief

## Personality
A brawl crosses one dangerous bridge between twin roofs while an industrial city falls away in every direction.

## Composition decisions

1. From every free-yaw gameplay-camera direction, the twin-roof shell remains the clearest silhouette: broad main roof, lower flank roof, and one narrow bridge across the alley.
2. From the main roof, the two-tier HVAC decks sit west of center as small, deliberate high ground; their simple stacked silhouette must remain readable without crowding the main-floor fight.
3. From the bridge and main-roof floor, the bridge is the sole hero connection and the visual commitment between roofs. The second roof and its penthouse read as flank/reset space, never as a second main arena.
4. Around every horizon, smaller industrial shafts, ramps, and building masses establish a continuous three-dimensional city. The world extends below the fight, rises above it, and continues at far distance rather than ending in backdrop panels.
5. Near façades and windows establish believable local depth at play distance. Their current gray-box masses are composition references only and must be replaced by approved real building assets while retaining their visual role.
6. The main combat floor, bridge, and HVAC route remain visually quiet enough that fighters, edges, and platform routes stay legible.

## Negative decisions

- No second hero landmark competes with the bridge.
- No clutter occupies the main fight routes, bridge, or HVAC access space.
- No retained gray-box façade/window treatment in production.
- No background geometry may become a safe-looking route, enter the blast corridor, or visibly intercept an ejected fighter. A player may attempt an unreachable building and cleanly blast out.

## Palette & lighting

Blue-hour dusk: cool industrial sky and metal, warm window accents, and enough value separation for roof silhouettes and fighters to read clearly.

## Camera vantages

- All four free-yaw quarter turns from the main-roof fight.
- Main-roof floor looking toward the bridge and second roof.
- Both HVAC deck heights looking across the main roof.
- The bridge crossing in both directions.
- Recovery and death-space looks toward the surrounding city.
